Landmark #1 • Marine Reserve

Giftun Islands

The Giftun Islands are one of the most celebrated natural treasures of the Red Sea. Surrounded by clear turquoise shallow waters, powdery white sand beaches, and thriving coral reef ecosystems.

Marine Wildlife: Home to sea turtles, tropical fish, and fringing reef systems.
Activities: Snorkeling, scuba diving, island sunbathing, and catamaran cruises.
Environment: Protected nature reserve preserving Egypt's Red Sea biodiversity.

Heritage & Heritage Eras

History & Historical Timeline

From an isolated bedouin fishing outpost of the Ababda tribe to a modern global tourist capital.

1905
1905

Founding of the Fishing Village

Hurghada was established near a natural harbor supported by local Ababda fishing communities. Life revolved around pearl diving, fishing, and trade across the Red Sea.

1913
1913

Discovery of Oil Deposits

Geological surveys discovered oil reserves in the coastal waters and desert perimeter, transforming Hurghada into an industrial outpost alongside its traditional fishing lifestyle.

1920s-1960s
1920s - 1960s

Industrialization & State Control

Crude oil extraction expanded rapidly. Under President Gamal Abdel Nasser during the 1950s, major oil extraction facilities were brought under state nationalization.

1970s
1970s

Dawn of Red Sea Tourism

Following regional peace agreements, international divers recognized Hurghada’s pristine underwater biodiversity. Basic resorts and diving centers began welcoming European enthusiasts.

1980s-Now
1980s - Present

Global Luxury Riviera

Unprecedented expansion brought luxury resorts, an international airport, marinas, and marine sanctuaries, solidifying Hurghada as Egypt's flagship Red Sea vacation hub.

Roman Era

Antiquity

Nearby Abu Sha'ar was fortified as a Roman military outpost and harbor, guarding trade routes between the Nile Valley and the Red Sea.

British Occupation

1882 – 1922

Hurghada remained a quiet fishing settlement while early geological expeditions mapped potential mineral wealth along the Red Sea coast.

Egyptian Kingdom

1922 – 1952

Sustained expansion in oil drilling and maritime fishing transformed the isolated settlement into a permanent coastal township.

Nasser Era

1956 – 1970

Industrial facilities and oil extraction fields were nationalized, placing strategic coastal economic sectors under state control.

Sadat Era

1970 – 1981

Peace and stability opened the door for commercial foreign investment, sowing the seeds of Red Sea international dive tourism.

Modern Era

1981 – Present

Exponential growth in international resorts, infrastructure, eco-conservation, and tourism hosting millions of visitors annually.

Attire & Warm Hospitality

Cultural Etiquette

Locals often wear traditional loose Galabiya robes suited for coastal desert breezes, while local Bedouin women craft vibrant embroidered scarves. Visitors are welcomed warmly in cafes and bazaars with fragrant Arabic mint tea and dark coffee.

National Marine Reserve

Giftun Islands Expedition

Giftun Island (Mahmya and Paradise beaches) is celebrated worldwide for crystal-clear turquoise waters and soft sandy shores. Boat trips leave daily from Hurghada Marina.

Waterfront Promenade

Hurghada Marina Boulevard

Featuring a pedestrian harbor walk with over 100 berths for private yachts, global cuisine restaurants, ice cream parlors, and live acoustic evening performances.

Islamic Heritage

El Mina Mosque Architecture

Situated adjacent to the old fishing port and marina, El Mina Mosque accommodates thousands of worshippers and features majestic marble domes, carved archways, and twin 40-meter minarets.
